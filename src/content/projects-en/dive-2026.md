## Problem

DIVE 2026 Global Data Hackathon (Busan Metropolitan City · Busan Technopark, Jul 25–26 at BEXCO), Track 5: **Busan Facilities Corporation × Wheelchair**. We were given call data from Duribal, the paratransit service for wheelchair users, and the question was: "Where do mobility-impaired people want to go, and where are they blocked?"

## Approach

We combined Duribal destination demand, floating population, and barrier-free infrastructure (facilities, accessible shops) on two aggregation units: **administrative districts and H3 grids**.

- Aggregated destination demand at H3 resolution 9 and extracted de-identified hotspots with DBSCAN.
- Built functional clusters of destinations (medical, administrative, commercial, …) with KMeans and tested which purposes were significantly skewed relative to the general population.
- Defined an **access-exclusion index** for places with demand but insufficient accessible infrastructure. Across the 18 districts of Haeundae-gu: Songjeong −0.99, Jwa-4 +1.16, Bansong-2 +1.52.
- Cross-checked against measured curb heights to produce 10 sites that diverge from current standards, 15 field-check candidates, and budget candidates with verified 300 m gaps.

The pipeline verifies key figures at the end of every run (e.g. 43,891 destination requests in Haeundae) and fails if any differ, so the numbers on the slides and in the code never disagree. Results went into an 8-menu Leaflet dashboard.

## Result

**3rd place** in the track and the **Busan Technopark President's Award**. After the event we wrote and submitted a separate track-extension proposal.

## What I learned

- *Defining* one index took longer than the modeling, and it was the part that drew the most questions in the presentation.
- With reproducibility checks bolted onto the end of the pipeline, we could fix numbers all night without re-checking the slides.
